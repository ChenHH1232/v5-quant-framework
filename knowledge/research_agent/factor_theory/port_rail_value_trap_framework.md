## Why Cheap Port / Rail Stocks Can Be Traps

Port and railway operators can look cheap by PB, PE, dividend yield or cash-flow yield while the underlying infrastructure economics are weakening.

## Main Traps

| Trap | Signal | Research interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throughput decline | falling revenue growth, falling cargo / freight volume | Asset utilization is weakening. |
| Dividend illusion | high dividend yield but weak free cash flow | Dividend may not be sustainable. |
| Capex burden | high capex / OCF | Cash is consumed by expansion or maintenance. |
| Leverage pressure | high asset-liability ratio or low interest coverage | Debt service may dominate shareholder return. |
| Business impurity | logistics, property, finance, investment or unrelated revenue | Port / rail cash-flow thesis may not apply. |
| Tariff pressure | regulated price cut or fee pressure | Revenue stability can break despite volume. |
| Regional concentration | single port / corridor exposure | Local trade or industrial cycle can dominate. |

## V5.5b Guard Candidate

V5.5b does not implement a hard guard yet.

It adds an operating-state score as a soft factor. The guard can be considered later only if Quant evidence shows:

```text
weak operating state consistently predicts poor forward returns;
the rule is stable across port and rail subgroups;
the rule does not simply remove bad historical years after the fact.
```
