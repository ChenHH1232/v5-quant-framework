## Core Question

For utilities operating companies, the key investment question is:

```text
Can stable cash flow cover interest, capital expenditure, and dividends?
```

## Initial Economic Logic

Utilities can be suitable for value and dividend research because many operators own long-lived assets, generate recurring demand, and may operate under regulated or quasi-regulated return structures.

However, utilities are capital-intensive. High leverage and high capital expenditure are often normal operating features, not automatic defects. A simple "lower debt is always better" rule may remove companies whose leverage is economically supportable.

## Candidate Modules

- Valuation: PB, PE, EV / EBITDA, operating cash-flow yield.
- Dividend: dividend yield, payout ratio, dividend continuity, dividend coverage by operating cash flow.
- Profitability quality: ROE, operating margin, earnings volatility, ROIC where available.
- Cash flow: operating cash flow, free cash flow, capex intensity, operating cash flow / net profit.
- Debt-service capacity: interest coverage, debt / operating cash flow, cash flow after interest and capex.

## Expected Failure Modes

- Fuel-cost cycles can compress margins for thermal power operators.
- Tariff or policy changes can alter earnings quality.
- High capital expenditure can make accounting profit look stronger than distributable cash.
- Dividend cuts can turn high yield into a value trap.
- Asset injections, restructuring, or mixed businesses can distort comparability.

## Research Status

These are hypotheses for Research Agent preparation only. They require PIT data mapping, IC / RankIC testing, baseline comparison, ablation, rolling validation, and robustness checks before any strategy can become a formal candidate.
