## Core Thesis

Coal companies may support high-dividend and value investing because mature mining assets can generate large cash flow during favorable commodity cycles.

However, coal is not a stable utility. Profit, cash flow, and dividends can be highly sensitive to coal prices, production constraints, safety costs, environmental policy, transport costs, and power-sector demand.

The key coal question is:

```text
Are current dividend and valuation signals supported by cycle-adjusted cash generation rather than peak-cycle accounting profit?
```

## Why High Dividend May Work

High dividend can indicate:

- mature assets with limited reinvestment need;
- shareholder-return discipline;
- strong operating cash flow;
- SOE payout policy support;
- market underpricing of durable cash generation.

## Why High Dividend May Fail

High dividend can also indicate:

- profit-cycle peak;
- market expectation of future coal-price decline;
- dividend paid from temporary earnings;
- underinvestment before reserve depletion or safety capex;
- payout pressure from parent-company capital needs;
- dividend cut risk when coal price or volume weakens.

## Why Valuation Needs Cycle State

Low PB and low PE in coal are ambiguous.

They may mean:

- undervalued reserves and cash-flow assets;
- overly pessimistic commodity assumptions;
- high dividend yield with stable production.

They may also mean:

- expected earnings normalization after a coal-price spike;
- stranded-asset or policy risk;
- future capex burden;
- mine-life or reserve quality concerns.

Therefore, valuation factors should be tested both unconditionally and conditional on coal-price / inventory / output states.

## Cash Flow And Capex

Coal value signals should be supported by:

- operating cash flow;
- free cash flow where capex data is reliable;
- OCF / net profit;
- dividend / OCF;
- dividend / FCF;
- capex burden.

Free cash flow is economically attractive but may be noisy if capex is lumpy. Quant Validation Agent must test coverage and robustness before promoting it.

## External Coal-Cycle State

V5.2 must introduce external state:

- thermal coal price;
- coking coal price;
- coal inventory or output;
- coal-power spread proxy.

These variables should not be treated as stock-level alpha automatically. They first serve as state variables that explain when value and dividend factors are more or less reliable.

## Failure Modes

Key failure modes:

- high dividend selected after a profit peak;
- coal price collapses after signal date;
- policy price controls reduce margin;
- safety or environmental restrictions reduce output;
- capex cliff reduces future free cash flow;
- mixed coal-chemical companies dilute coal exposure;
- parent-company restructuring changes business economics;
- current industry classification creates survivor bias.

## Research Position

The initial V5.2 hypothesis is plausible but unproven.

It cannot become a formal strategy candidate until PIT validation shows that retained factors remain financially explainable, statistically stable, and robust across coal-cycle states.
