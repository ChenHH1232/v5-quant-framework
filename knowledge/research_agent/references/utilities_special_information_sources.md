## Why Utilities Need Sector-Specific Information

Utilities are not a homogeneous high-dividend basket. Even inside public utilities, thermal power, hydropower, nuclear power, gas, water and grid operators have different economic drivers.

Generic value factors such as PB and dividend yield are useful baselines, but they are not enough to explain why one utility subgroup should outperform another.

## Priority Information Layers

### 1. Power Operators

Relevant information:

- electricity tariff mechanism;
- coal / fuel cost;
- utilization hours;
- generation mix;
- regulated or marketized electricity exposure;
- capex cycle and new capacity;
- cash-flow stability.

Research use:

- thermal power should be tested with fuel-cost and tariff context;
- hydropower should be tested with water inflow and utilization context;
- nuclear power should be tested with capacity schedule and regulatory stability;
- renewable generation should remain separate from Test-1 unless a dedicated model is built.

### 2. Gas And Water Operators

Relevant information:

- regulated price adjustment;
- user growth;
- concession stability;
- pipeline / network capex;
- receivable pressure;
- cash conversion.

Research use:

- compare cash-flow yield and dividend sustainability within the same sub-industry;
- avoid treating all utilities leverage and capex as the same.

### 3. Dividend Sustainability

Relevant information:

- dividend yield;
- payout ratio;
- operating cash flow;
- capex requirement;
- free cash flow;
- debt-service pressure.

Research use:

High dividend should not be treated as standalone alpha. It needs cash-flow support.

### 4. External Data To Add Later

Potential data:

- coal price or fuel-cost index;
- power generation by source;
- utilization hours by source;
- electricity tariff / market transaction price indicators;
- water inflow or hydrology indicators for hydropower;
- gas price and regulated tariff updates;
- official power-sector statistics.

These fields should be added as macro / subgroup state variables, not as raw performance-tuned overlays.

## Source Types

Recommended source order:

1. Official statistics and regulator releases.
2. Exchange disclosures and company annual reports.
3. Industry association reports.
4. Academic papers and sell-side research for theory and risk taxonomy.
5. Market commentary only for hypothesis discovery.

```text
generic value + generic dividend + generic quality composite
```
